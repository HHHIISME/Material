Effect of La2O3 on Grain Refinement
and Thermal Conductivity of 6 mol % Y2O3–ZrO2 Fibers
Kangkang Yuana, Xiaotong Jina, Xinqiang Wanga, *, Guanghui Zhanga, Luyi Zhua, and Dong Xua
aState Key Laboratory of Crystal Materials and Institute of Crystal Materials, Shandong University, Jinan, 250100 P.R. China
*e-mail: xqwang@sdu.edu.cn
Received March 10, 2019; revised March 17, 2019; accepted March 26, 2019
Abstract—ZrO2 ceramic fibers have gained widespread study for the high temperature stability, low thermal
conductivity and high mechanical properties. While for oxide fibers, grain growth is one of the limiting factors
for long-term high temperature applications. In the present study, polycrystalline ZrO2–6 mol % Y2O3
ceramic fibers with the addition of ~0–5 mol % La2O3 to refine the grain size were fabricated by the dry-spin-
ning method. The XRD and Raman spectral results showed that the pyrochlore La2Zr2O7 phase was formed
as a second phase in the tetragonal zirconia fiber matrix. The formation of a second phase was an effective
way to hinder grain growth for the competition phase effect and solid solution mechanism. With the addition
of La2O3, the grain size decreased and concentrated in a smaller range of 50–250 nm compared with the
ZrO2–6 mol % Y2O3 matrix at 1400°C. Furthermore, the thermal conductivity of the fibers decreased with
the additive of La2O3 less than 3 mol %. The present system is a promising candidate for high temperature
applications.
Keywords: zirconia fibers, grain growth, pyrochlore, La2Zr2O7, thermal conductivity
DOI: 10.1134/S0036023619110226
INTRODUCTION
In the last decades, ceramic fibers have been
gained deep study and widespread use for the essential
components of new high-temperature-resistant light-
weight materials [1]. The energy crisis in 1973 pro-
moted the development of ceramic fibers in high-ther-
mal insulating area for the low thermal conductivity
and light weight properties. Furthermore, due to the
high thermal shock-resistance and high toughness of
ceramic fibers, the ceramic matrix composites
(CMCs) have obtained further study for the promising
utility in advanced fields, such as spaceflight and mil-
itary affairs [2]. For the high melting point, good
chemical stability, high temperature stability, low
thermal conductivity, high strength and toughness [3],
yttria stabilized zirconia (YSZ) fiber has become a
promising candidate for ultra-high temperature appli-
cations.
Different methods have been used to prepare YSZ
fibers. The template impregnation method by soaking
inorganic salts in collagen fiber was used to fabricate
the porous structure but with low strength [4]. Dry
spinning technique combined with sol-gel method was
used for the easy way and mass production [5], which
is an attractive method in industry. Recently, electro-
spinning method has been used to prepare the ultra-
thin zirconia fibers with the diameter range from
50 nm to 1000 nm with high surface area and lower
conductivity [6–8]. However, larger grains tend to
form for oxide fibers when used at high temperatures
over a long period of time. At the expense of smaller
grains through the diffusion process at grain boundar-
ies, grain growth made the ceramic fibers brittle and
even pulverized. What is more, polycrystalline oxide
fibers are prone to creep under load at high tempera-
tures which made the ceramic fibers impossible for
long time application in CMCs. Enhanced creep
properties and reduced grain growth rates are focused
on the development of oxide fibers for current
research activities [1, 9]. From the previous work, it
has been found that the grain size could be reduced by
the competition between matrix phase and the addi-
tive phase [10]. Furthermore, smaller grain size and
more defects generated by the added phase could lead
to phonon scattering [11, 12], decreasing the thermal
conductivity of the matrix. The second phase forma-
tion would be an effective way to improve the tensile
strength and thermal conductivity of ceramic fibers.
In the present work, dry-spinning combined with
sol-gel method was used for the fabrication of ZrO2–
6 mol % Y2O3 (6YSZ) ceramic fibers. La2O3 was
added to the 6YSZ ceramic fibers for hindering the
grain growth. The effect of 0–5 mol % La2O3 addition
on the grain growth and microstructure of zirconia
fibers was studied. As thermal conductivity is an
important parameter for high thermal insulating, the

EXPERIMENTAL
Materials
Basic zirconium carbonate (BZC, ZrO2 ≥ 40%,
Zibo Xinlüyuan Biological Chemical Co., Ltd.), gla-
cial acetic acid (CH3COOH, ≥99.5%, Tianjin Fuyu
Fine Chemical Co., Ltd.), anhydrous methanol
(CH3OH, ≥99.5%, Tianjin Fuyu Fine Chemical Co.,
Ltd.), and yttrium nitrate hexahydrate Y(NO3)3 ·
6H2O, ≥99.5%, Aladdin Chemistry Co., Ltd.) were
used as starting materials without further purification.
Preparation of Fibers by Dry-Spinning Method
Zirconia fibers were prepared through a dry-spin-
ning method based on our previous study [5]. Basic
zirconium carbonate (50 g) was mixed with glacial
acetic acid (25 g) in methanol (25 mL) with stirring at
60°C until the solution became transparent, and then
Y(NO3)3 · 6H2O (7.93 g) was added to the above solu-
tion to get zirconia fiber (6YSZ) precursor solution.
According to above method, different amount of
La(NO3)3 · 6H2O, BZC and glacial acetic acid were
added to the 6YSZ precursor solution to get the 1 mol %
La2Zr2O7–6YSZ (1LYSZ), 2 mol % La2Zr2O7–6YSZ
(2LYSZ), 3 mol % La2Zr2O7–6YSZ (3LYSZ), 4 mol %
La2Zr2O7–6YSZ (4LYSZ), 5 mol % La2Zr2O7–6YSZ
(5LYSZ) precursor solutions. The precursor solutions
were concentrated and dry-spun with a rotating speed
of 12000–18000 rpm to get the precursor fibers. The
obtained precursor fibers were heat-treated at the rate
of 1 K/min to 600°C, and then to 1400°C at the rate of
2 K/min and held for 2 h at 1400°С to get the final
fibers.
Characterization
Raman spectrum was obtained by means of a
LabRAM HR800 spectrometer equipped with a CCD
detector at room temperature and a 632.8 nm laser as
excitation. X-ray diffraction (XRD, D8 Advance,
Bruker, Germany) data collection was performed with
CuKα radiation using a graphite monochromator in
the range of 10°–90°. Scanning electron microscope
(SEM, S-4800, Hitachi, Japan) was used to observe
the surface morphologies and microstructures of the
fibers. The fibers were grinded with 5% PVA aqueous
solution and infused in a mold to obtain a pellet (4 × 4 ×
1 mm) for each sample. Pellets was put into a furnace
at 1000°C for 2 h to completely remove the PVA and
then used for thermal conductivity test. Thermal con-
ductivity was measured by using a NETZSCH
LFA457 instrument (LFA457, NETZSCH, Ger-
many). The density and porosity of the pellets were
measured by the Archimedes method and shown in
Table 1.
RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
Structure and Phase Characterization
Figure 1 shows the XRD patterns of zirconia fibers
mixed with different amount of La2O3 heat treated at
1400ºC. For 6YSZ, the obvious splitting peaks at 2θ =
34°–36° and 59°–61° and major peaks in the figure
suggest the formation of tetragonal phase of zirconia
for the sample. With the increasing additive of La2O3,
the main characteristic peaks which are in agreement
with JCPDS (17-0450) standard for pyrochlore phase
of La2Zr2O7 arise and become higher. In the previous
study, La2Zr2O7 can exist as a crystalline fluorite phase
of a cubic structure of an intermediate compound or
a pyrochlore phase for heat treatment at different tem-
peratures [13].
Above 1200°C, the pyrochlore structure was the
pure phase for the sol-gel method when the stoichio-
metric ratio of La2Zr2O7 was mixed [14]. In the present
work, fibers heat treated at 1400°C leads to the forma-
tion of pyrochlore La2Zr2O7, which is the result for the
reaction of La2O3 precursor with ZrO2 precursor.
In order to improve the explanation of the XRD
results and confirm the phase composition, Raman
spectra of the fibers were collected and shown in Fig. 2.
According to the structural properties and group the-
ory, the tetragonal zirconia shows six Raman active
fundamentals, while the monoclinic zirconia has
18 peaks and the cubic phase keeps only one [15]. For
La2Zr2O7, six Raman active modes are predicted for
the pyrochlore structure although two bands are
always indistinguishable from the background in the
spectra [16] and only one Raman mode for the fluorite
structure [17]. The obvious differences in Raman

Six peaks were detected in the 6YSZ sample in Fig. 2

the polycrystalline fibers in the Raman spectra, which
was in consistence with the XRD results.
Morphologies and Microstructures of the Fibers
The morphologies and microstructures of the sur-
face for 6YSZ and 3LYSZ fibers were characterized by
SEM and exhibited in Fig. 3. Seen from Fig. 3, one
can know that the additive of La2O3 and the formation
of La2Zr2O7 obviously hindered the grain growth of
the fibers.
In order to clearly study the effect of La2Zr2O7 on
the grain size of the fibers, more than 500 grains were
collected for each sample and the distribution fre-
quency of the grains less than 200 nm were shown in
Fig. 4. For zirconia fibers without the dopant of
La2O3, the grain size distribution less than 200 nm was
76.1%, and the small amount of larger grains could
induce the abnormal grain growth which could deteri-
orate the strength of the fibers. While with the additive
of La2O3, the grain size distribution was decreased and
much more grains were concentrated on a smaller
grain size range less than 200 nm.
The additive of La2O3 and formation of La2Zr2O7
not only lead to the competition between YSZ and
La2Zr2O7 phases in grain growth which can effectively
reduce the grain size [10] but also as a solid solution
which can inhibit the grain growth by the mechanism
of solid drag [18]. Combined with the SEM images of
the fibers in Fig. 3, small amount of La2O3 dopant can
obviously decrease the grain size of the fibers and
increase the concentration of the grain size in the
range less than 200 nm which may lead a higher per-
formance for the fibers.
Thermal Conductivity of the Fibers
Figure 5 shows the results of the thermal conduc-
tivity of the pellets after heat-treated at 1000°C. The
thermal conductivity of all the samples has an increas-
ing tendency with the temperature increased. As the
dopant of La2O3 was less than 3 mol %, the thermal
conductivity of the pallets was lower than that of
6YSZ. While for higher additive of La2O3, the thermal
conductivity was higher than that of 6YSZ.
According to the previous study, four factors can be
considered for the influence on the thermal conduc-
tivity: (a) grain size; (b) porosity; (c) vacancy; (d) lat-
tice distortion [19]. For polycrystalline ceramics,
larger grains lead to higher thermal conductivity for
zirconia [20]. In general, if the pore shape and distri-
bution are neglected, the porosity can effectively lower
the thermal conductivity because the pallets can be
simply described as a binary mixture model of tetrag-
onal zirconia and pores. Furthermore, the thermal
conductivity of a polycrystalline material at tempera-
ture lower than 800°C can be described by the expres-

CONCLUSIONS
In summary, the influence of 0–5 mol % La2O3
additive on the grain size and thermal conductivity of
zirconia fibers was investigated. The addition of La2O3
which reacted with the ZrO2 leads to the formation of
La2Zr2O7 as a second phase in the zirconia matrix. In
comparison with 6YSZ fibers, the second phase addi-
tion can hinder the grain growth of the fibers, decrease
the grain size and increase the concentration of grain
size distribution. Furthermore, the thermal conduc-
tivity of the fibers was decreased with the additive
amount of La2O3 less than 3 mol %. This can be
ascribed to the formation of phonon scattering cen-
ters, which reduced the phonon mean-free path.
