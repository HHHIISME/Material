Abstract.
Micro-laminated ZrO2/Al2O3 interlayer was
prepared between sol-gel ﬁber-reinforced thermal barrier
coating (TBC) and Ni-based superalloy by cathodic elec-
trodeposition. Micro-laminated ZrO2/Al2O3 interlayer was
composed of ZrO2 and Al2O3 alternating layers, which
demonstrated excellent oxidation resistance.
Scratching
and cyclic oxidation tests were performed to evaluate
the bonding strength and oxidation resistance of the sol-
gel TBCs with micro-laminated ZrO2/Al2O3 interlayers.
The results indicated that the bonding strength between
TBCs and Ni-based superalloy was improved by the micro-
laminated ZrO2/Al2O3 interlayer; and the oxidation resis-
tance of Ni-based substrate was signiﬁcantly improved due
to the interlayer This kind of micro-laminated ZrO2/Al2O3
interlayer may have potential application as the bond coat
in TBCs systems.
Keywords.
Sol-gel thermal barrier coatings, micro-
laminated ZrO2/Al2O3 interlayers, bonding strength, ox-
idation resistance, Ni-based superalloy.

Introduction
Thermal barrier coatings (TBCs) are widely used to provide
thermal insulation in gas-turbine engines, power generation,
and marine propulsion [1–4]. Temperature gradients at the
order of 100 to 150ıC can be achieved by TBCs on inter-
nally cooled turbine parts [5]. TBCs system typically con-
sists of an yttria stabilized zirconia top coat and a metallic

bond coat deposited onto a superalloy substrate. Thermally
grown oxide (TGO) predominantly composed of ˛-Al2O3
will form at the interface between the bond coat and top
coat via oxidation of the bond coat during the operation of
the gas turbine.
At an industrial scale, plasma spraying (PS) and electron
beam physical vapor deposition (EB-PVD) are the main
processes to fabricate thermal barrier coatings. Different
microstructures are obtained from these two methods [6]:
lamellar microstructure consisting of splats superposition
for PS thermal barrier coatings and column-like microstruc-
ture for EB-PVD thermal barrier coatings with best me-
chanical performances. However, the perpendicular orien-
tation of columns makes their thermal conductivity twice
higher than PS coatings. In recent years, sol-gel process
as a non-directional deposition technique has been devel-
oped for preparation of TBCs [7–9]. This is a versatile and
cost-effective method for preparing ceramic coatings. The
main advantage of sol-gel TBCs is that they can form non-
oriented microstructures which show satisfactory thermo-
mechanical properties when the coating subjects cyclic ox-
idation [10] However, since the sol-gel deposition is a wet-
chemical technique, the coating is prone to cracking and
spallation especially when the thickness of the coating in-
creases to a certain level. The fabrication condition should
be precisely controlled to obtain the crack-free coatings.
With respect to the bond coat, MCrAlY (where M D
Ni, Co or both) metallic coatings are used extensively dur-
ing the past decades. A selective ˛-Al2O3 protective scale
(TGO) could form on the metallic coating under high tem-
perature operation. However, MCrAlY alloy coatings have
some weaknesses which limit their further application. On
one hand, high stresses are usually presented in the inter-
facial zone between the top coat and bond coat due to the
growth of TGO, thermal expansion mismatch between dif-
ferent materials and applied loads [5]. As the result, cracks
can initiate and propagate, which may cause spallation of
the ceramic TBC layer.
On the other hand, it has been
found that the alloying elements such as Al and Cr in the
bond coat have a severe inter-diffusion with the superalloy
substrate above 1273 K, resulting in depletion of Al and Cr
and deterioration of oxidation resistance of the coating [11].
Moreover, some alloying elements such as Ti, Mo and W in
the bond coat may also diffuse outwards and weaken the ad-
hesion of TGO to the substrate [12]. Therefore, people look

In the present paper, we introduce a micro-laminated
ZrO2/Al2O3 interlayer into the sol-gel ﬁber-reinforced

interlayer on the bonding strength and oxidation resistance

Experimental Procedure
Commercial GH864 Ni-based superalloy (sample size 15 
10  3 mm3/ was used as the substrate material. The chem-
ical composition of the Ni-based substrate is listed in Ta-
ble 1. The substrates were mechanically polished by SiC
papers to a grit of 1500 followed by ultrasonically cleaned
with deionized water and ethanol.
Micro-laminated ZrO2/Al2O3 interlayers were prepared
on Ni-based superalloy substrates by cathodic electrolytic
deposition (ELD). ZrO2 and Al2O3 layers were deposited
onto the substrates alternately. The deposition time of each
layer was 60 s and the interlayer has a total of 6 thin layers
(3 layers of ZrO2 and 3 layers of Al2O3/.
The yttria partially stabilized zirconia TBCs were pre-
pared through a sol-gel route. A measured amount of zir-
conium nitrate and yttrium nitrate were ﬁrst dissolved in
deionized water, followed by adding citric acid (CA) and
ethylene glycol (EG) into the solution. The molar ratio of
Zr4C : CA : EG was 1 W 1 W 20. The zirconia sol was
obtained by adding ammonia water to reach pH 7.
Af-
ter that, ZrO2–6 wt% Y2O3 powders with a mean size of
0.5 μm (Hot Spraying Material Ltd., China) were added
into the sol, and Al2O3–10 wt% ZrO2 ceramic ﬁbers were
also added into the sol in order to increase the strength of
the sol-gel TBCs. The molar ratio of ZrO2 powders/ZrO2
in the sol and Al2O3–ZrO2 ﬁbers/total ZrO2 were 7=1 and
1=9, respectively.
Al2O3-ZrO2 ceramic ﬁbers were produced by an immer-
sion method as reported in a previous paper [13] Cotton
ﬁbers were used as template and aluminum nitrate solu-
tion (with 10 wt% zirconia nitrate) was used as immer-
sion solution.
After immersion, drying and sintering at
1200ıC, Al2O3–ZrO2 ceramic ﬁbers were ﬁnally obtained.
The composite slurry was then coated onto the alloy sub-
strates with or without micro-laminated ZrO2/Al2O3 inter-
layers. After drying at 80ıC for 15 min and sintering at
600ıC under a pressure of 9 MPa, the green coatings were
microwave sintered at 1000ıC for 20 min to obtain the ﬁ-
nal coatings. The microwave frequency was 2450 MHz and
the average power of the microwave furnace was 2 KW, ap-
proximately
The surface and cross-section morphology of the coat-
ings and post-oxidation specimens were observed by a ﬁeld
emission SEM (SUPRA 55 ZEISS, Germany) equipped
with an energy dispersive spectrometer (EDS). Phase iden-
tiﬁcations were conducted by X-ray diffraction (CuK˛,  D
0:15406 nm, step wise of 0.02°, continuous scanning). The
bonding strength between TBCs and substrate was evalu-
ated by a MFT-4000 surface properties tester with a loading
rate of 20 N=min and scratch length of 5 mm. Cyclic ox-
idation test was conducted in a tube furnace at 1000ıC in
air for 150 h. After every 10 h exposure, specimens were
taken out and cooled in air for 20 min. The mass gain and
scale spallation were weighed using an electronic balance
with an accuracy of 0.1 mg.

3.3
Effect of Micro-laminated ZrO2/Al2O3 Interlayer on
Oxidation Resistance
Figure 4 shows the photographs of specimens with vari-
ous coatings before and after cyclic oxidation at 1000ıC
for 15 h. It can be seen that before oxidation, Ni-based
superalloy substrate exhibited metal luster after polishing,
and the ﬁber-reinforced thermal barrier coatings with and
without ZrO2/Al2O3 interlayer were all crack-free, and well
integrated to the substrates. After oxidation, however, a
black-color, thermally grown oxide (TGO) formed on the
surface of the blank sample. Comparing the specimens with
and without ZrO2/Al2O3 interlayer, it can be seen that a se-
vere spallation took place on the TBC without interlayer,
whereas the TBC with ZrO2/Al2O3 interlayer was still in-
tact on the substrate after 150 h cyclic oxidation.
Figure 5 shows the oxidation kinetic curves of speci-
mens with various coatings after oxidation at 1000ıC for
15 h. Both specimens with TBCs exhibited a lower mass
gain than the blank sample due to the suppression of oxy-
gen inter-diffusion by the thermal barrier coating. In com-
parison of the TBCs with and without ZrO2/Al2O3 inter-
layer, the interlayer specimen showed a better oxidation re-
sistance than that without ZrO2/Al2O3 interlayer, which can
be attributed to the oxidation resistance of micro-laminated
ZrO2/Al2O3 interlayer.

For TBC without ZrO2/Al2O3 interlayer, a TGO layer with
the thickness of 20 μm was formed beneath the coating.
A large and continuous crack can be seen above the TGO
layer, indicating that the vicinity of TGO was the weakest
place during high temperature oxidation. Micro-cracks can
also be found in the TGO layer; and internal oxidation took
place under TGO.
In contrast, TBC with ZrO2/Al2O3 interlayer was well-
adhered to the superalloy substrate and no cracks formed in
the coating. Compared with the interlayer morphology be-
fore oxidation as shown in Figure 1(d), no layered morphol-
ogy of TGO formed between the interlayer and alloy sub-
strate. However, a certain degree of internal oxidation in the
alloy substrate occurred after oxidation, which can be ex-
plained from the property of ZrO2/Al2O3 interlayer and the
nature of the Ni-based superalloy substrate. As Al2O3 has
HCP crystal structure and low diffusion rate [14], it widely
used as the diffusion barrier to suppress the inter-diffusion
between alloy coating and alloy substrate. The ZrO2/Al2O3
interlayer can play a similar role that suppresses the out-
ward diffusion of alloy elements, and probably convert the
diffusion from predominant outward metal ion diffusion to
inward oxygen diffusion.
As the inward oxygen diffusion results in a relatively low
oxygen partial pressure in the interlayer/substrate interface
due to the low diffusion rate of oxygen in Al2O3, it will lead
to a low parabolic rate constant and thus reduce the criti-
cal concentration of Al to form Al2O3 in the alloy substrate
based on the Wagnar’s selective oxidation theory [15]. So a
thermally grown Al2O3 layer tends to form at the interface
between the interlayer and substrate. However, as the Ni su-
peralloy substrate has a relatively low concentration of Al
(see Table 1), which still cannot reach the critical concentra-
tion for selective oxidation even with the decreased critical
concentration of Al. Therefore, internal oxides formed as
shown in Figure 7(c).

Discussion
There are two aspects of strengthening effects that should
be considered in a coating/substrate system as shown in
Figure 8. One is self-strengthening effect of the coating
which can improve the cracking resistance, and the other is
the bonding-strength improvement which is related to the
thermal expansion coefﬁcients and interface structure. For
TBCs prepared by sol-gel route, it is an effective way to
add high-strength ceramic ﬁbers to the sol slurry to enhance
their strength which has been demonstrated in our recent re-
search. But the bonding-strength also needs improvement
especially when it is coated directly onto the Ni-based su-

Micro-laminated ZrO2/Al2O3 coatings have been proved
to have excellent oxidation and scale spallation resistance
on alloy substrates based on our previous works [16, 17].
They have two main effects on this TBC system: (1) their
good oxidation resistance can slow down oxidation re-
action of the substrate and avoid formation of cracks in
the interlayer/substrate interface; and (2) their good adhe-
sion makes the weak bonding position changed from the
TBC/substrate interface to TBC/interlayer interface and the
thermal stress is reduced due to their relatively close ther-
mal expansion coefﬁcient. Therefore, this kind of micro-
laminated ZrO2/Al2O3 interlayer may be used as the bond
coat for TBCs system

Conclusions
The present work studies the oxidation resistance of sol-
gel ﬁber-reinforced TBCs on Ni-based superalloys. Micro-
laminated ZrO2/Al2O3 interlayers were applied between the
TBCs and Ni-based superalloys. The effect of the interlayer
can be concluded below:
(1) The oxidation resistance of TBCs can be signiﬁ-
cantly improved due to the addition of micro-laminated
ZrO2/Al2O3 interlayers.
(2) Micro-laminated ZrO2/Al2O3 interlayers not only
avoid the formation of ﬂaws or cracks in the inter-
layer/substrate interface but also reduce the thermal expan-
sion difference between TBCs and the substrate thus en-
hancing the bonding strength and spallation resistance of
TBCs.
(3) This kind of micro-laminated ZrO2/Al2O3interlayer
may have potential applications as bond coat in TBCs sys-
tems.
