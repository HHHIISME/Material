ZrO2:xEr3+,yYb3+ ﬁbers (diameters of approximately 1 mm) with good ﬂexibility and toughness were
synthesized
via
electrospinning
technology.
The
composition,
morphology,
and

1.
Introduction
Up-conversion luminescence is the process of combining low-
frequency excitation photons into a high-frequency emission
photon. Since its discovery, various uorescence materials have
been studied for the development of three-dimensional displays,1
temperature sensors, uorescence dyes,2 clean energy,3 biomed-
ical imaging, therapeutics,4–6 photodynamic therapy methods,7
lasers,8 and white light sources.9 Nanoparticles (NPs) and nano-
crystals (NCs) comprise the largest fraction of up-conversion
materials.10
For example, RE3+, Er3+, Tm3+ and Ho3+, have abundant
ladder-like electron energy level structures and intra-4f transi-
tions, making them be excellent activators inside host materials.
However, Er3+, Tm3+ and Ho3+ have very narrow absorption
cross-sections in the near-infrared, leading to singly doped
materials that have very low luminous eﬃciencies. Yb3+ ions
have only two energy levels (4F7/2 and 4F5/2), and the gap of the
two energy levels is agreement with a 980 nm CW laser, which
means that Yb3+ has a large absorption cross-section and a high
quenching concentration (17–30 mol%). The excited Yb3+ ions
can transfer energy to adjacent Er3+, Tm3+, or Ho3+, promoting
activators into an excited state. Therefore, Yb3+ is an excellent
sensitizer within up-conversion luminescence materials. Thus
far, the search for up-conversion phosphors has mainly focused
on nanophosphors based on functional nanocomposites.11
These include uorides such as La(Gd,Ce)F3:Yb3+,Ho3+/Nd3+/
Tb3+,6
Ba(Sr)F2:Yb3+,Ln3+(Tm3+/Nd3+),12

such as YVO4:Yb3+,Er3+,19,20 or oxides, such as Y2O3:Yb3+,Er3+/
Li+,21 La2O3:Yb3+,Er3+,14 Gd2O3:Yb3+,Er3+ among others.22,23
Since the discovery of carbon nanotubes,13 many reports
have focused on one-dimensional nanomaterials, such as
nanorods, nanowires, nanobelts, and nanobers,24 which
possess great potential for addressing some basic issues about
dimensionality and space-conned transport phenomena as
well as practical applications.25,26 Some researchers4,27–29 have
reported fabricating ZrO2:Tb3+/Er3+/Tm3+/Ho3+,Yb3+ NPs/NCs.
However, these NPs/NCs tend to aggregate to some extent,
and their simple shape and construction limits their production
and application.30 Sometimes, additional modications are
needed to attenuate aggregation.27 Incorporation of functional
NPs/NCs into electrospun bers or mats could endow the
materials better performance in applications using the self-
assembly of nanoscale building blocks. Additionally, electro-
spinning methods generally do not require surface functional-
ization and can realize high yields.31
Among inorganic compounds, oxides possess good chemical
and thermal stability, as well as high mechanical stability.1

2.
Experimental methods
2.1.
Synthetic procedures
All chemical reagents used were of analytical grade. Zirconia
bers doped with Er3+,Yb3+ ions were synthesized with electro-
spinning technology. Firstly, polyethylene oxide (PEO, MW 
1 300 000, 0.075 g) was dissolved in methyl alcohol (20 mL)
under
magnetic
stirring
at
55
C.
Secondly,
poly-
acetylacetonatozirconia (PAZ, 7.5 g) was introduced into this
solution under magnetic stirring at 35 C. Additional methyl
alcohol was added to adjust the concentration to 0.2–0.25 g
mL1 to form a transparent and homogeneous hybrid solution.
The spinning solution was aged for 12 h to form precursor ZrO2
for electrospinning. Thirdly, the as-prepared precursor solution
was exposed to an applied voltage of 17.5 kV with a 20-gauge
plastic syringe using a ow rate of 2 mL h1. The resulting bers
were collected on aluminium foil covering a rotating drum,
keeping the distance between the collector and spinneret at
20 cm. Finally, the ZrO2 precursor bers obtained were pre-
heated at 500 C for 1 h using a very slow heating rate in steam
to remove extraneous organic materials. Then, the bers were
sintered at 1000 C for 1 h in air. Based on the amount of ZrO2
bers synthesized, diﬀerent stoichiometric amounts of erbium
nitrate [Er(NO3)3$5H2O] and ytterbium nitrate [Yb(NO3)3$6H2O]
were added into the spinning solution. Similarly, the ageing
precursor solution underwent electrospinning, pre-heating, and
sintering until the nal ZrO2:xEr3+,yYb3+ (x ¼ 0.05–0.6 mol%, y
¼ 1–20 mol%) bers were obtained.
2.2.
Characterization
The scanning electron micrographs (SEM) of the as prepared
bers were acquired by a Hitachi S-4800 eld emission scanning
electron microscope, and elemental analysis measurement was
obtained using an energy dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS,
Horiba EMAX Energy, EX-350). The X-ray diﬀraction (XRD)
patterns of all the samples were recorded by a Germany Bruker
Axs D8-Avance diﬀractometer system with a graphite mono-
chromatized Cu Ka irradiation (l ¼ 1.540598 ˚A). The up-
conversion emission spectra of the samples were recorded
with an Edinburgh FLS 980 Fluorescence Spectrometer using an
external 980 nm semiconductor solid laser as the excitation
source. All measurements were performed at room temperature.

Fig. 3(a) displays the up-conversion emission spectra of doped
ZrO2 bers under excitation of 980 nm as a function of the
concentration of Er3+ ions. The emission spectra can be attrib-
uted to the characteristic emission of Er3+ ions, in particular, its
intra-4f9 multi-electron transitions. The emission up-conversion
PL intensity of the three emission peaks increases with the
doping concentration, which is consistent with energy radiating
primarily from transitions from 2H11/2/4S3/2 to 4I15/2. Thus, the
green emission has a higher intensity. However, the emission
intensity decreases with increasing Er3+ concentration increasing
when the doping level is higher than 0.2 mol%, as shown in
Fig. 3(b). That could be explained by the distance between Er3+
ions being too close to lead to concentration quenching because

quenching of emission takes place at higher concentrations of
Er3+ ions. As a result, the optimal Er3+ doping concentration is 0.2
mol%. To understand the energy transfer and concentration

The rst involves an activator Er3+ ion absorbing a photon to
reach an excited state. However, the absorption coeﬃcient of
this process is relatively low, with an eﬃciency about one tenth
than of Yb3+, mainly because these ions have a much larger
absorption cross-section and could transfer energy eﬃciently to
Er3+ ions. The second is energy transfer up-conversion (ETU), in
which, the sensitization by Yb3+ ions allows Er3+ ions to be
excited via energy transfer. The energy diﬀerence between the
2F7/2 and 2F5/2 levels of Yb3+ corresponds to the energy of
a 980 nm CW laser. As shown in Fig. 8, Yb3+ ions can absorb

Conclusions
Color-tunable of up-conversion photoluminescence in ZrO2:-
xEr3+,yYb3+ ber cloths were prepared via electrospinning
technology. Under 980 nm excitation, ZrO2:0.2 mol% Er3+ bers
could emit green light. The addition of Yb3+ ions can enhance
the emission intensity, and change the ratio of red to green
light. The main up-conversion mechanism of ZrO2:xEr3+,yYb3+
was energy transfer from Yb3+ to Er3+ ions, accompanied by
cross relaxation. When the concentration of Yb3+ was 20 mol%,
the ratio of red to green was maximized, with only red emission
observed.
