Despite La2Zr2O7 ceramic ﬁber has been fabricated by electrospinning method, the effects of thermal
pyrolysis process on the microstructures and properties of the ﬁbers have seldom been considered. Three
La2O3 precursors were used to prepare La2Zr2O7 ceramic ﬁbers. Phase transformation of La2Zr2O7 ceramic
ﬁbers were characterized by XRD and Raman spectra. The inﬂuence of the decomposition process on the
microstructure of the ﬁbers was studied by TG/DSC, XPS and SEM. The results show that slow weight loss
leads to smaller grain size which could obtain higher strength ﬁbers while fast weight loss could develop
pores which are beneﬁt to the decrease of the thermal conductivity.

1. Introduction
The pyrochlore phase of La2Zr2O7, owning the properties of
high
melting
point
(2300 °C),
low
thermal
conductivity
(1.56 W m1 K1), good thermal and chemical stability, no phase
transitions, and low sintering activity, is believed to be a promising
candidate for the high temperature insulation applications [1]. Due
to the
high
temperature
stability and
pyrochlore
structure,
La2Zr2O7 can also be used as support in the catalytic combustion
[2] and host of ﬂuorescence center [3]. Furthermore, the ferro-
electric [4] and electrical properties [5] of La2Zr2O7 were also
studied for its superstructure.
Electrospinning, a process that could stretch a viscous con-
ductive ﬂuid into a thin jet via electrostatic force, is a simple
method of fabricating ﬁberous materials with diameters ranging
from several micrometers to tens of nanometers, including poly-
mer, composite and ceramic [6]. Combining with the sol-gel
method, inorganic nanoﬁbers could be produced by the calcina-
tion of precursor ﬁbers at high temperatures. La2Zr2O7 ﬁbers can
be fabricated by electrospinnig with sol-gel method. There have
been several reports on the preparation of La2Zr2O7 ﬁbers with
electrospinning [2,5,7], while the loose structure and large crys-
talline grain size at 1000 °C would lead to a poor strength of the
ﬁbers. Dense La2Zr2O7 ﬁbers have been fabricated by Tang et al.
[8], but the doped silica would limit the high temperature
application.
In the present work, LaCl3  7H2O, La(NO3)3  6H2O, and La
(CH3COO)3  4H2O were used as La2O3 precursors to prepare pure
La2Zr2O7 ﬁbers via electrospinning method. The inﬂuence of the
three La2O3 precursors on the ﬁber microstructure was char-
acterized by SEM and TG/DSC. The phase transformation process
and
thermal
conductivity
of
the
La2Zr2O7
ﬁber
were
also
discussed.

The obtained precursor ﬁbers were heated at the rate of
2 °C/min to different temperatures and held for 2 h when ﬁbers
were treated above 600 °C. Thermogravimetry and differential
scanning calorimetry (TG/DSC, SDT Q600 v8.3 Biuld 101, TA, US)
measurements of the precursors were performed at a heating rate
of 10 °C/min from room temperature to 800 °C in air. Fourier
transformation infrared (FT-IR, ALPHA-T, Bruker, Germany) spec-
trum recorded in the region 4000–400 cm1 was measured with a
RT-DLATGS spectrometer using the KBr pellet method. Raman
spectrum was obtained by means of a LabRAM HR800 spectro-
meter equipped with a CCD detector at room temperature and a
632.8 nm laser as excitation. Elemental analysis of the samples
was undertaken, using X-ray photoelelctron spectroscopy (XPS,
Thermo Fisher scientiﬁc, ESCALAB250, US) and Al–Kα radiation.
X-ray diffraction (XRD, D8 Advance, Bruker, Germany) data col-
lection was performed with Cu–Kα radiation using a graphite
monochromator in the range of 10–90° with the increment of
0.02° and 0.08 s/step. Scanning electron microscope (SEM, S-4800,
Hitachi, Japan) was used to observe the surface morphologies and
microstructures of the ﬁbers. The ﬁbers treated at 1200 °C were
grinded with 5% PVA aqueous solution and infused in a mold to
obtain a pellet (4 mm  4 mm  1 mm) with a uniaxial press of
8 MPa. The pellet was put into a furnace at 1000 °C for 2 h to
completely remove the PVA and then used for thermal con-
ductivity test. Thermal conductivity was measured by using a
NETZSCH LFA457 instrument (LFA457, NETZSCH, Germany), and
the value at each point was the average of triplicate analysis with
standard derivation less than 0.01 W m1 K1. The density and
porosity of the pellets were measured by the Archimedes method
and shown in Table 1.

3.2. Formation of pyrochlore La2Zr2O7 ﬁbers
The crystalline structures of the three precursor ﬁbers were
examined by XRD which were shown in Fig. 4. La2Zr2O7 is con-
sidered as pyrochlore-type thermodynamically stable structure for
the ratio of ionic radii of cations La/Zr¼1.61 [18] larger than 1.46
[19]. The anion vacant makes the surrounding anions to relax from
the ideal position in ﬂuorite, leading to an observable super-
structure in the anionic array [20]. As the superstructure of
pyrochlore
formed,
superlattice
diffraction
peaks,
such
as
2θ¼36.2° (331), 43.5° (511), can be used to distinguish the defect
ﬂuorite from the pyrochlore. According to the XRD patterns in
Fig. 4, the diffraction peaks indexed to the ﬂuorite structure ap-
peared at 800 °C. It is conﬁrmed that pyrochlore phase start to
form at 1000 °C because of the obvious pyrochlore superstructure
reﬂections, i.e., 2θ¼36.2° (331) and the less obvious 43.5° (511)
etc. At 1200 °C, the pyrochlore superstructure reﬂections became
more apparent and intense, which suggests the well crystallized
and pure pyrochlore phase.
Raman spectroscopy was further used to characterize the
crystalline structure because it is more sensitive to the metal-
oxygen mode. For the vacancy in the pyrochlore structure, defect
ﬂuoride structure can be easily distinguished by Raman spectra.

Raman mode (F2g) for the ﬂuorite structure [11]. There are only
one broad band for the three precursor ﬁbers sintered at 800 °C in

3.3. Microstructure and morphology evolution of the ﬁbers
SEM micrographs of NLZ, CLZ, ALZ ﬁbers sintered from 1000 °C
to 1400 °C were shown in Fig. 6. At 1000 °C, uniform crystalline
surface was obtained for CLZ ﬁber. Compared with the non-crys-
talline smoother section for NLZ ﬁber and the stripe line surface of
ALZ ﬁber, the surface differences may be due to the thermal
etching for the uneven organic pyrolysis. At 1200 °C, surface pores
appeared for NLZ and ALZ ﬁbers, while dense microstructure was
obtained for CLZ ﬁber. According to the thermal pyrolysis process,
much solid carbonaceous residual was generated for the rapid and
deﬁcient organic oxidation pyrolysis. Gas was produced by the
residual oxidation at high temperatures, thus leading to the pores
in NLZ and ALZ. Hence, slow organic decomposition was beneﬁt to
the dense microstructure formation. At 1400 °C, pores were
eliminated through mass transportation and grain boundary mi-
gration during grain growth. The average nanocrystalline size for

3.4. Thermal conductivity of the ﬁbers
The thermal conductivity of the samples was shown in Fig. 8.
With the increasing of temperature, the thermal conductivity in-
creases for the three samples, which shows an adverse tendency
compared with fully dense polycrystalline La2Zr2O7 ceramic [24].

CLZ pallet was much higher than that of ALZ and NLZ pallets,
which shows the experimental thermal conductivity of the present
samples strongly depends on the porosity. Although the porosity

4. Conclusion
LaCl3  7H2O, La(NO3)3  7H2O, and La(CH3COO)3  4H2O were
used as three different La2O3 precursors for the fabrication of
La2Zr2O7 ﬁbers via electrospinning method. The phase transfor-
mation of La2Zr2O7 from defect ﬂuorite to pyrochlore starts at
about 1000 °C. The TG/DSC and SEM results show that the pyr-
olysis of the precursor ﬁbers has as obvious impact on the mi-
crostructure. The slow and continuous weight loss of CLZ leads to
formation of smaller grain size ﬁber than those of NLZ and ALZ,
which could obtain higher strength ﬁbers for the small grain dis-
tribution. Fast weight loss could develop pores which are beneﬁt
to increase the porosity and decrease the thermal conductivity,
although pores could be eliminated at higher temperatures for NLZ
and ALZ ﬁbers.
